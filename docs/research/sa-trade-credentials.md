# Which regulated credentials do the eight launch categories actually require?

Facts for [What must be true before an Artisan can Quote, and what does a badge prove?](https://github.com/sfxpers/artisanconnect/issues/76). Not legal advice. National South African law only. Cape Town is the city the brief proposes, and it is not locked, so a city by-law is recorded as a boundary, not as a launch credential.

Researched 24 September 2026 against the statute, the regulator, or the statutory body's own rules. A blog, a training provider, or a trade association is not cited as evidence that a credential is required.

## How to read a "none"

A "none" means the statutes and regulators checked do not require a named credential before a person may do that work. It does not mean the work is unregulated. Building work still has to meet the National Building Regulations. Construction work still has health and safety duties. A trade certificate can still be issued for the occupation. Issuing a certificate is not the same thing as forbidding the work without one. That prohibition exists, in the sources checked, only where a statute says so: plumbing, electrical installation work, and gas installation or removal.

## Plumbing

Regulation A18 of the National Building Regulations says no person shall perform the trade of plumbing as contemplated in Government Notice R. 1875 of 31 August 1979 unless he is a trained plumber, or works under the adequate control of a trained plumber or an approved competent person. Doing the trade without being a trained plumber, and without being a person grandfathered under an old local-authority registration, is an offence. A trained plumber who lets an untrained person practise the trade without adequately controlling the work also commits an offence. A local authority may not, for the purposes of these regulations, register any person to practise the trade of plumbing after the Act came into operation. Older local registrations may continue where another local authority accepts them.

Source: National Building Regulations, regulation A18, made under the National Building Regulations and Building Standards Act 103 of 1977, published under Government Notice R. 2378 in Government Gazette 12780 of 12 October 1990. Read from the consolidated text, including the 2008 amendments, hosted by the City of Cape Town document centre: [NATIONAL BUILDING REGULATIONS 2008](https://resource.capetown.gov.za/documentcentre/Documents/Procedures,%20guidelines%20and%20regulations/NATIONAL%20BUILDING%20REGULATIONS%202008.pdf). The same five subregulations are set out at [Acts Online, A18](https://www.acts.co.za/national-building/r2378_a18__control_of_plumbers).

"Trained plumber" means a person who, in the trade of plumbing, has passed a qualifying trade test or been issued a certificate of proficiency in terms of the Manpower Training Act 56 of 1981, or has obtained a National Certificate in Construction Plumbing at NQF level 3. The NQF limb was added by Government Notice R. 574 in Government Gazette 31084 of 30 May 2008. Same consolidated text, definition of "trained plumber".

The credential attaches to the person. The regulation does not say it expires, and it does not require renewal, a card, or membership of a board.

The Manpower Training Act route is the historical one. Section 26D(4) of the Skills Development Act 97 of 1998, inserted by the Skills Development Amendment Act 37 of 2008, now requires the Quality Council for Trades and Occupations to issue a trade certificate to a person who has passed a trade test, stating that the person is qualified to perform the trade named on the certificate. The QCTO says it took over trade certification from the Department of Higher Education and Training on 1 October 2013, and that it issues replacements for certificates previously issued under the Manpower Training Act. Sources: [Skills Development Amendment Act 37 of 2008, section 26D](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Acts/Skils%20Development%20Act/Amendments%20Acts/skilldevact.pdf), and the [QCTO page for learners](https://www.qcto.org.za/for-learners.html). Section 26D does not say the certificate expires.

There is a naming gap. The building regulation still names the National Certificate in Construction Plumbing at NQF level 3. SAQA records that qualification, ID 21853, as replaced. The replacement is the Occupational Certificate: Plumber, SAQA ID 91782, NQF level 4. SAQA says 91782 prepares a learner to install, maintain, test and repair hot and cold water supply systems, drainage, sewerage and rainwater systems, and that it covers the recorded plumber trades. Sources: [SAQA 21853](https://allqs.saqa.org.za/showQualification.php?id=21853) and [SAQA 91782](https://regqs.saqa.org.za/viewQualification.php?id=91782). The regulation text has not been amended to name 91782. A legacy trade test or certificate of proficiency still sits inside the definition as written. A current QCTO plumbing trade certificate is the successor instrument. It is not, on the face of A18, a differently worded third limb.

Government Notice R. 1875 of 31 August 1979 is the notice that defines the trade A18 controls. It was a designation of the trade under the old manpower-training legislation. The notice text was not retrieved from a gazette in this pass. Searches for "R. 1875" and "31 August 1979" plumbing trade returned the cross-reference in A18, not the notice itself. The scope of the prohibition is therefore "the trade of plumbing" as that notice defined it, not every job a person might call plumbing. The current SAQA occupational certificate, above, is the closest official description of the occupation, and it is not that 1979 notice.

The Water Services Act 108 of 1997, section 9(1)(e), lets the Minister prescribe compulsory national standards on "requirements for persons who install and operate water services works." Source: [Water Services Act](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a108-97.pdf). That is an enabling power. The in-force standards instrument commonly cited under the Act, Government Notice R. 509 of 8 June 2001, is described by the Department of Water and Sanitation as a metering and conservation standard, not as a plumber's licence. See the Department's citation of R. 509 in [Government Notice 982 of 2017](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201709/41100gon982.pdf). A 2013 draft under the same section would have registered process controllers at water services works. It is a draft, and it is about treatment works, not domestic plumbing: [Government Notice 813 of 2013](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/36958rg10040gon813.pdf). The sources checked do not show a national plumber's licence made under the Water Services Act.

### PIRB is not the statutory credential

The Plumbing Industry Registration Board is a professional body recognised by the South African Qualifications Authority. SAQA classifies it as non-statutory. Recognition runs from 10 May 2022 to 9 May 2027 under decision SAQA 08143/24. Registered designations include Licensed Plumber and Qualified Plumber. Source: [SAQA professional-body record 831](https://pbdesig.saqa.org.za/viewProfessionalBody.php?id=831).

SAQA recognition does not, by itself, make registration a condition of doing the work. No statute checked requires PIRB registration, a PIRB certificate of compliance, or Institute of Plumbing South Africa membership before a person may plumb. A18(3) points the other way for building-regulation purposes: a local authority may not keep its own plumber register for those regulations. PIRB's own renewal cycle, assessments, and certificate of compliance are rules of a non-statutory body. They are a voluntary register, even where an insurer or a municipality prefers them.

### Gas is a separate gate, and only for gas work

If the plumbing job is the installation or removal of a gas appliance, a gas system, or a gas reticulation system, a different statute applies. Regulation 17(1)(c) of the Pressure Equipment Regulations, 2009, says no person shall do that unless the person is an authorised person. An authorised person or an approved inspection authority must issue a certificate of conformity after a gas installation, modification, alteration, or change of user or ownership.

"Authorised person" means a person registered as competent within the scope of work for which an organisation approved by the chief inspector has registered that person. The regulations do not name the organisation and do not set an expiry.

Sources: Pressure Equipment Regulations, Government Notice R. 734 in Government Gazette 32395 of 15 July 2009, regulations 1 and 17. Official publication: [gov.za notice](https://www.gov.za/documents/notices/occupational-health-and-safety-act-regulations-pressure-equipment-15-jul-2009), file [32395734.pdf](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/32395734.pdf). That file is image-only. The quoted text was read from a reproduction that carries the same gazette masthead: [SAQCC Gas copy of GN R. 734](http://saqccgas.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/pressure_equipment_regulations.pdf).

The Department of Labour's guidance notes, Government Notice 167 in Government Gazette 38505 of 27 February 2015, identify the approved organisation for this definition as including "SAQCC (Gas) registered gas practitioner in the applicable field." Source: [Guidance notes](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201503/38505gon167.pdf). SAQCC Gas says it is a non-profit company mandated by the Department of Employment and Labour to register gas practitioners for natural gas, liquefied petroleum gas, air-conditioning and refrigeration gas, and compressed industrial and medical gases, and that registration is compulsory under the Pressure Equipment Regulations. Source: [About SAQCC Gas](https://saqccgas.co.za/about-saqcc-gas/).

The regulations do not say the registration expires. The approved organisation does. Its refrigeration category page says registration is valid for three years. Source: [SAQCC Gas, SARACCA registration category](https://saqccgas.co.za/gas-practioners/registration-categories-per-assoc/saracca-assoc/registration-category-saracca-2/). That page is one gas speciality. It is evidence of the organisation's cycle, not a term written into the regulation. The registration attaches to the person, within a scope of work.

Ordinary water and drainage plumbing is not this credential. A fixed electric hot-water storage container of 15 to 450 litres, operating at a maximum of 600 kPa and manufactured to SANS 151, is excluded from the Pressure Equipment Regulations, and must be installed to SANS 10254. Source: regulation 2(3)(h) of the same Government Notice R. 734. Replacing that kind of geyser is plumbing under A18, not gas-practitioner work, unless the job is also a gas installation.

## Electrical

The Electrical Installation Regulations, 2009, were made under section 43 of the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993. Government Notice R. 242 in Government Gazette 31975 of 6 March 2009. They came into effect on 1 May 2009. Source: [eir2009.pdf on labour.gov.za](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Regulations%20and%20Notices/Regulations/Occupational%20Health%20and%20Safety/eir2009.pdf).

They regulate electrical installation work, not every job that could be called electrical. An electrical installation is machinery, in or on premises, used to transmit electricity from a point of control to a point of consumption, including articles that form part of it. It excludes the supplier's own supply machinery, communication and television or radio circuits, an installation on a vehicle, vessel, train or aircraft, and control circuits of 50 V or less between parts of a unit that are separately installed and derived from an independent source or an isolating transformer. Installation work means installing, extending, modifying or repairing such an installation; connecting machinery at its supply terminals; or inspecting, testing and verifying an installation in order to issue a certificate of compliance. Appliance repair that is none of those things is outside these regulations.

### Registered person: the person

Regulation 5(4): a registered person shall exercise general control over all electrical installation work being carried out, and no person may allow such work without that control. "General control" includes instruction, guidance and supervision.

Regulation 9(1): no person other than a registered person may issue a certificate of compliance. Regulation 9(4): any person who undertakes to do electrical installation work shall ensure that a valid certificate of compliance is issued for that work.

A registered person is a natural person registered under regulation 11, or under regulation 9 of the 1992 regulations, as one of three categories:

- Electrical tester for single phase: verification and certification of installations supplied at single phase at the point of control, excluding specialised electrical installations.
- Installation electrician: the same for any electrical installation, excluding specialised electrical installations.
- Master installation electrician: the same for any electrical installation, which is the category that covers specialised installations. Specialised installations are those in explosive atmospheres (SANS 10086-1), the petroleum industry (SANS 10089-2), hazardous locations (SANS 10108), and medical locations (SANS 10142-1).

Regulation 11: application is made to the chief inspector. The chief inspector registers a natural person who satisfies the chief inspector that the person has sufficient knowledge of the applicable rules and appropriate practical experience, and furnishes a certificate of registration. The certificate must be produced on request. The regulations do not give the certificate an expiry date. Regulation 12 lets the chief inspector withdraw it if the person no longer meets regulation 11(2), or is convicted of an offence under regulation 15, after notice and a chance to comply.

The Department's own explanatory notes, Government Notice 258 in Government Gazette 35180 of 26 March 2012, say: "All certificates of registration issued since 24 May 1982 are still valid, unless it has been cancelled or withdrawn." Source: [explanatory notes](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/35180gen258.pdf). Those notes guide; they do not replace the regulations. They are the regulator's statement that the personal certificate does not expire by effluxion of time.

Where the voltage exceeds 1 kV, regulation 5(5) adds a design approval. That approval must come from a person who is competent under paragraph (b), (c) or (d) of the definition of competent person in the General Machinery Regulations, 1988, or from a person registered in a professional category under the Engineering Profession Act 46 of 2000. That is a design credential for the high-voltage part. It is not a substitute for the registered person, and it is not required for ordinary low-voltage installation work.

The Electrical Contracting Board, or ECB, is not this issuer. The same explanatory notes say that from 1 June 2012 contractor registration is done at the nine provincial offices, and that an ECB registration remained valid only until it lapsed. ECB registration is not a current statutory credential.

### Electrical contractor: the undertaking

Regulation 6(1): no person may do electrical installation work as an electrical contractor unless registered as an electrical contractor. An electrical contractor is a person who undertakes to perform electrical installation work on behalf of any other person. The definition excludes an employee of that person. So the employee on the tools is not the contractor. The person or business that takes on the job is.

Regulation 6(2): a person who does the work as an electrical contractor shall register annually with the chief inspector, or a person the chief inspector appoints, on Annexure 3. Regulation 6(4): the chief inspector must register the applicant if the applicant has a fixed address and a telephone, and employs a registered person full-time, or is a registered person. The registration is entered on a national database.

The Department's current Annexure 3 says the applicant may be a sole proprietor, partnership, company or close corporation, asks for the business registration number, and says the fee is "R120 per year up to three years (The electrical contractor may register for a maximum period of three years)." Source: [Annexure 3 application form](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Forms/Occupational%20Health%20and%20Safety/Application%20form%20for%20Electrical%20Contractor%20registration%20-%20Electrical%20Installation%20Regulations%20Annexure%203%20with%20banking%20details%20and%20addresses.pdf). The annual duty is in regulation 6(2). The three-year maximum is on the regulator's form, not in regulation 6 itself.

A solo artisan who undertakes installation work for a customer therefore needs both: registration as a registered person, which does not expire unless withdrawn, and registration as an electrical contractor, which is renewed. An employee does not need the contractor registration. Someone who is not a registered person may do the work only under the general control of one, and may not issue the certificate of compliance.

SANS 10142-1, the wiring code, is incorporated as a safety standard by Government Notice R. 243 of 6 March 2009. Source: [incorporation notice](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Regulations%20and%20Notices/Regulations/Occupational%20Health%20and%20Safety/Electrical%20Installation%20Regulations,%202009%20-%20Standards%20incorporated.pdf). It is the standard the installation must meet. It is not a second personal licence.

## Carpentry and cabinetry

No statute or regulator checked requires a credential before a person may do carpentry or cabinetry.

Checked:

- The National Building Regulations, Parts A to X, in the consolidated text cited above. The only regulation that controls who may perform a named trade is A18, and it names plumbing. There is no equivalent for carpentry, joinery, or cabinetmaking.
- The Occupational Health and Safety Act regulations that create a personal or contractor registration for a trade: the Electrical Installation Regulations and the Pressure Equipment Regulations. Neither names carpentry.
- Section 26D of the Skills Development Act, which provides for a trade certificate stating that the holder is qualified to perform the named trade. It does not prohibit an uncertificated person from doing the work.
- The SAQA professional-body register, which was checked for a statutory body whose registration is a condition of carpentry. The plumbing record, cited above, is expressly non-statutory. No statutory carpentry body turned up as a precondition to the work.

A trade test in carpentry, if held, is a qualification. It is not, on these sources, a licence to work.

## Painting

No statute or regulator checked requires a credential before a person may paint.

The same instruments were checked as for carpentry. The National Building Regulations do not contain a "control of painters" regulation. The Occupational Health and Safety Act regulations checked do not register painters. A hazardous-chemical duty, where it applies to a substance, is a duty about the substance. It is not a painter's licence, and no such licence was found.

Searches for a statutory painter registration, a painter's practising certificate, or a statutory painting board returned trade-association and training-provider pages. Those are not sources for a legal requirement, and they are not cited as evidence that one exists.

## Tiling

No statute or regulator checked requires a credential before a person may tile.

Same instruments as carpentry and painting. The National Building Regulations regulate floors and walls as building elements. They do not name a tiler, and they do not require a tiler to be registered. No Occupational Health and Safety Act regulation checked creates a tiler registration. No statutory tiling body was found whose registration is a condition of doing the work.

## Brickwork and plastering

No statute or regulator checked requires a credential before a person may lay bricks or plaster.

Part K of the National Building Regulations requires walls to have structural strength and stability, to resist water, and to behave appropriately in fire. Those are performance requirements. They are not a bricklayer's or plasterer's licence. Where a rational design or rational assessment is used, regulation A19 requires the appointment of a person responsible for design, inspection and assessment. "Competent person" in these regulations means a person qualified by education, training, experience and contextual knowledge to make a determination about the performance of a building or part of it, or to undertake duties assigned under the regulations. That appointment is about the design or assessment. It is not a requirement that the person who lays the bricks hold a named credential. Sources: the consolidated National Building Regulations cited above, Part K, regulation A19, and the definition of "competent person" as substituted by Government Notice R. 574 of 2008.

The Engineering Profession Act 46 of 2000 is the statute under which a person registered in a professional category practises engineering. The Electrical Installation Regulations invoke it for the design of an installation above 1 kV, as noted above. Nothing in the building regulations or the occupational-health regulations checked requires a bricklayer or plasterer to be registered with the Engineering Council of South Africa in order to do the trade. Identified-engineering-work regulations were not read line by line in this pass. What was read does not place an ECSA registration on the bricklayer.

## Roofing

No statute or regulator checked requires a credential before a person may roof.

Part L of the National Building Regulations requires a roof to be designed and constructed so that it performs: it must resist the actions it will meet, resist fire as required, and meet the deemed-to-satisfy route where that route is used. Source: the consolidated regulations cited above, Part L. There is no "control of roofers" regulation, and no Occupational Health and Safety Act regulation checked registers roofers.

Thatch, waterproofing, and fall-arrest duties were not found to create a personal roofer's licence. A search for a statutory roofing registration returned industry and training pages, not a statute or a regulator rule. Those pages are not cited as evidence of a requirement.

## Welding and metalwork

No statute or regulator checked requires a personal credential before a person may do general welding or metalwork, such as gates, frames, burglar bars, or non-pressure fabrication.

The Pressure Equipment Regulations do regulate one slice of welding. "Repair" means restoration to original standard by the application of heat or welding to any pressure equipment, or the replacement of expanded tubes. The user must ensure that repaired or modified pressure equipment has a certificate issued by the repairer or modifier, with a verification signature by an approved inspection authority when required. Source: Government Notice R. 734 of 15 July 2009, definition of "repair", and regulation 6(2)(c), in the gazette reproduction cited under plumbing. That is a duty on the repair of pressure equipment. It does not say "no person shall weld unless registered as a welder." There is no welder category alongside "authorised person." Authorised person, in these regulations, is the gas, inspection, and fire-extinguisher registration described above, not a general welding licence.

The Southern African Institute of Welding was not found to be established by a statute that forbids welding without its certificate. Searches for a statutory welder registration returned the institute and training providers. Those are not a statutory credential, and they are not cited as one. A contract or an incorporated construction standard may still require a qualified welding procedure for a particular job. That is a standard for the work, not a licence to be a welder.

## Not a trade credential

These are real statutory registrations. They do not belong in the table as a credential of Plumbing, Electrical, Carpentry, Painting, Tiling, Brickwork, Roofing, or Welding. They attach to a different activity.

### Home builder, NHBRC

Section 10(1) of the Housing Consumers Protection Measures Act 95 of 1998: no person shall carry on the business of a home builder, or receive any consideration from a housing consumer in respect of the sale or construction of a home, unless that person is a registered home builder. Section 10(2): no home builder shall construct a home unless registered. A home is a dwelling unit constructed or to be constructed by a home builder, after the Act commenced, for residential purposes or partially for residential purposes, excluding a category the Minister prescribes. A home builder is a person who carries on the business of a home builder, or an owner-builder who has not been exempted. Source: the Act as published by the National Home Builders Registration Council, [Housing Consumers Protection Measures Act](https://www.nhbrc.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/2.1.1-Housing-Consumers-Protection-Measures-Act-95-of-1998.pdf).

The Council is the issuer. Registration attaches to the home builder, who may be a person or a business carrying on that business. The NHBRC says that, in terms of the Act, registration must be renewed annually. Source: [NHBRC renewal](https://nhbrc.org.za/renewal/). The Council's rules provide for expiration and non-renewal of registration. Source: [NHBRC Rules](https://www.nhbrc.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/2.2.2-NHBRC-Rules.pdf), rule 2.

This is the credential for constructing a home as a home builder. It is not a credential for painting a room, tiling a floor, or repairing a pipe in an existing home. An artisan who is the home builder constructing the dwelling needs it. An artisan who is not carrying on that business does not, on section 10.

### Public-sector construction contractor, cidb

Section 18(1) of the Construction Industry Development Board Act 38 of 2000: a contractor may not undertake, carry out or complete any construction works, or portion thereof, for public sector contracts awarded by competitive tender or quotation, unless registered with the Board and holding a valid registration certificate. Section 20(1): registration is valid for three years. Source: [Act 38 of 2000](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a38-000.pdf).

The cidb says the register applies to contractors who wish to tender for public-sector construction, and that home building is outside its scope and falls under the NHBRC. Source: [cidb mandate](https://www.cidb.org.za/about-us/our-construction-mandate/). The Construction Industry Development Regulations exempt a contractor registered as a home builder, for construction works that are the provision of a home, and exempt a contractor whose work is substantially the provision of labour. Source: the cidb's [Practice Note 13](https://www.cidb.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Practice-Note-13.pdf), quoting regulation 4 of those regulations. Practice Note 13 is the Board's note. The prohibition itself is section 18 of the Act.

Registration attaches to the contractor, not to each artisan. It is not required for a private home job. A draft amendment published for comment on 3 May 2024 would extend the register to private-sector procurement. It is a draft bill. It says it comes into operation on a date fixed by the President. Source: [Government Notice 2448 in Government Gazette 50608 of 3 May 2024](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202405/50608gen2448.pdf). It is not in force. The cidb's own mandate page, cited above, still describes a public-sector register.

### Construction health and safety appointments

The Construction Regulations, 2014, Government Notice R. 84 in Government Gazette 37305 of 7 February 2014, are published by the Department of Employment and Labour: [ConstructionRegulation_2014.pdf](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Regulations%20and%20Notices/Regulations/Occupational%20Health%20and%20Safety/ConstructionRegulation_2014.pdf). The gazette file is a scan and was not extracted line by line. A Department conference paper quotes regulation 8(6) as prohibiting appointment of a construction health and safety officer unless that officer is registered with a statutory body approved by the chief inspector. Source: [Department of Employment and Labour, professional-bodies presentation](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Publications/Occupational%20Health%20and%20Safety/OHS%20Construction%20Conference%202024%20-%20Presentations/Professional%20Bodies%20and%20Professional%20Designations%20in%20the%20Built%20Environment%20_Saiosh%20Presentation.pdf). That appointment is a site health-and-safety role. It is not a licence to carpenter, paint, tile, lay bricks, roof, or weld. Draft Construction Regulations published for comment in 2025 are not in force. Source: the Department's [12 March 2025 notice](https://www.labour.gov.za/department-of-employment-and-labour-invites-public-comments-on-the-repeal-of-construction-regulations).

### Bargaining councils

A bargaining council registration, where a collective agreement has been extended, is an employment-law duty of an employer in that industry and area. It was not treated as a credential to perform any of the eight trades. No bargaining-council certificate was found that functions as a licence to do the work.

## Municipal by-laws are outside this table

City is not locked, and the question asked for national law. A municipality can still add a local gate, especially for a connection to its water or electricity system. Two illustrations, not launch rules:

The Electrical Installation Regulations' own explanatory notes say a supplier's by-laws are subordinate and may not add to or detract from those regulations, though a supplier may regulate matters outside the definition of an electrical installation, and matters that safeguard the supplier's distribution system. Source: [explanatory notes](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/35180gen258.pdf), the notes immediately before the commentary on regulation 6.

The City of Cape Town water by-law, as amended in 2018, says no person who is not qualified and accredited in terms of the National Qualifications Framework Act 67 of 2008 may do specified installation work, replace a fixed water heater or its protective devices, inspect and test certain installations, or service a backflow preventer. It also lets a plumber who is so qualified register with the City, and the City keeps that register. Sources: [Water By-law, 2010, section 47](https://resource.capetown.gov.za/documentcentre/Documents/Bylaws%20and%20policies/Water%20By-law%202010.pdf) and [Water Amendment By-law, 2018, sections 46A and 47](https://resource.capetown.gov.za/documentcentre/Documents/Bylaws%20and%20policies/Water%20Amendment%20By-law%202018.pdf). That is a municipal rule for Cape Town water-installation work. It is not a national plumber's licence, and A18(3) still forbids a local authority from registering plumbers for the purposes of the National Building Regulations. Another city's by-law may say something else, or nothing. It should not be treated as a fact about the eight categories until a city is locked.

## What was not found

For carpentry and cabinetry, painting, tiling, brickwork and plastering, roofing, and general welding and metalwork, the sources checked do not show a statutory credential. The searches were for a statute, a regulator, or a statutory body requiring registration or a certificate before a person may do that work. They were run against the National Building Regulations, the Occupational Health and Safety Act trade-registration regulations, the Skills Development Act's trade-certificate provision, the SAQA professional-body register, the Construction Industry Development Board Act, and the Housing Consumers Protection Measures Act. Industry registers and training-provider pages that appeared in those searches were not used as evidence of a legal requirement.
